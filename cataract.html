Cataract

Cataract is a commonly occurring vision problem that develops as you grow older, caused by a gradual clouding of the eye's natural crystalline lens from a build-up of proteins. Left untreated it progressively blurs your vision — but it is also one of the most treatable causes of vision loss.

Overview

What Is a Cataract?

The eye's natural lens sits just behind the iris and focuses light onto the retina. Over time, proteins within the lens begin to clump together, gradually clouding an area of the lens — this clouding is a cataract. As it grows denser, less light reaches the retina and vision becomes progressively blurred, dim or hazy.

Cataracts usually develop slowly and are most often related to ageing, though they can occasionally be present from birth or triggered by injury, certain medications or other eye conditions. Cataract surgery — replacing the clouded natural lens with a clear artificial intraocular lens (IOL) — is one of the safest and most commonly performed surgeries in ophthalmology.

Symptoms

Recognising the Early Signs

Cataract develops gradually, so symptoms are often subtle at first. Watch for these common signs.

Cloudy or Blurred Vision

Vision gradually looks hazy, foggy or less sharp, even with an updated glasses prescription.

Glare & Halos Around Lights

Increased sensitivity to bright light and rings of light around headlights or lamps, especially at night.

Difficulty Seeing at Night

Driving or moving around after dark becomes noticeably harder as low-light vision declines.

Fading or Yellowing of Colours

Colours appear less vivid, slightly faded or take on a yellow-brown tinge.

Frequent Prescription Changes

Needing new glasses or contact lens power more often than before, without vision improving much.

Double or Distorted Vision

Seeing a ghosted second image or distorted shapes, sometimes noticeable in just one eye.

Causes

What Increases the Risk?

Cataract is primarily an age-related change — most patients develop one after the age of 60, as part of the eye's natural ageing process. In India alone, an estimated 20 lakh new cataract cases are diagnosed every year. Certain health conditions and lifestyle factors can also speed up how quickly a cataract forms.

Treatment

Treatment Options

Cataract surgery replaces the clouded natural lens with a clear intraocular lens (IOL). Several surgical techniques and lens types are available.

Phacoemulsification

The standard modern technique — ultrasound energy breaks up the clouded lens through a tiny 2.4–2.8mm incision, which is then removed and replaced with a folded IOL. Quick, stitchless and widely performed.

Learn More

MICS — Micro-Incision Cataract Surgery

An even smaller 2.2–2.4mm incision for faster visual recovery and minimal astigmatism, ideal for select premium IOL implants.

Learn More

FLACS — Femtosecond Laser-Assisted Surgery

A bladeless, laser-guided option that performs key surgical steps with computer precision instead of a handheld blade, offering a highly customised, less-invasive experience.

Learn More

IOL Options — Monofocal to EDOF

From monofocal and astigmatism-correcting lenses to multifocal and extended depth-of-focus (EDOF) IOLs — your surgeon helps you choose the lens that best fits your lifestyle and vision goals.

Learn More

Not sure which lens is right for you?

Our specialists will walk you through every IOL option based on your lifestyle, vision needs and eye health.
